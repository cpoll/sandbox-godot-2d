# Testing
Test methodology:
    1. Open up and pin the Debugger -> Profiler and click Autostart
    2. Run the game until the bullets hit the bottom and start despawning
    3. Observe the Frame Time after 30s.


Tests are done with 50 bullet rows and a spawn rate of 0.1seconds unless specified.

Number | Conditions | Frame Time (ms)
---
1 | Naive | 16-24
2 | Naive w/ collision layers | 15-17
3 | Above, with 25 bullet rows | 4-7
4 | queue_free disabled | constantly increasing
5 | pooling | 10-13ms
6 | collision disabled | 10-13
7 | area2d deleted | 5
8 | area2d not monitoring | 10-13
9 | spawner moves bullets | 10-13
10 | 0.05 bullet frequency | 177
11 | dumb bullet | 12
12 | dumb bullet with area2d | 140
13 | circle collider | 177
14 | multiple moves | 140
15 | dumb hand-rolled collision | 10
16 | distance_to instead of distance_to_squared | 10
17 | shader | 6
18 | screen space shader | 6
19 | mesh2d | 6
20 | smart bullets | 6-8

# Test Notes

Test 2/3: Seems obvious enough that we're close to dropping frames, and the naive approach
isn't good enough. Still, 50 rows spawning every 0.1 seconds is a lot of bullets,
more than I think you see in a danmaku (I should add a bullet count).
But even 4ms seems like a long time for this.

Test 4: Commented out queue_free(). The flame graph shows a lot of time spent on 'Cull CanvasItem Tree'. I think that's
deciding which elements don't need to be rendered (should be culled). I think this increases linearly
with number of elements in the scene. Commenting out queue_free() suggests as much, as it increases
linearly (or some low order exponential) with time running. Does multimesh bypass this?

Test 5: Added pooling. Lower time, but I wonder if that's just an artifact. It also makes the bullets a bit
janky; I might not be properly resetting them.
I now see the Visual Profiler isn't
what I want. The regular profiler has further breakdowns. Physics2D: 3ms, Script functions (Bullet._process): 3.76ms.
Question: how do you break down the Profiler "Process Time" into individual steps?

Test 6/7: Turning off the bullet's layer does a bit, but deleting the Area2Ds and CollisionShape2Ds completely eliminates the Physics2D profiler entry.
Physics2D is otherwise 3ms of Integrate Forces. This seems like a strange thing, I thought integrate forces was for movement. I suppose Area2D has some extra crap.

Test 8: Disable Bullet's Area2D.Monitoring. Seems to do nothing, probably identical to turning off all Collision masks.

Test 9: Remove the script from each bullet. Instead, the bullet spawner moves and disables the bullets. This should remove the 3.76ms of Bullet._process, and hopefully
not just move it to bullet_spawning._process. This should also remove Physics2D. We'll have to reimplement collisions later.
Actual result: Script's up to 5ms, mostly move_bullets. despawn_bullet is sometimes 3ms as well, so Array.erase() isn't the best.

Test 10: Played with the bullet counts. We're at 2800 for the above experiments. When we hit 5000~ we get exponentially slower, move() goes up to 80ms. I wonder why.
It doesn't look like the slowdown is in despawn_bullet(), it's all a move() problem...

Test 11: Switch to dumb bullet (no script, no area2d). Seems to work. Which is interesting, why is moving so much more expensive. Is it because it has to move its children
with it, and do some Area2D stuff?

Test 12: Add a useless Area2D to the dumb bullet. It's back to being slow. That seems to be the problem, not just moving the object. So it's a bit deceptive that the problem
was in move_bullets. I guess it recalculates the collision immediately when you change position?

Test 13: Back to regular bullet, try a circle collider (bullet and player). Doesn't help.

Test 14: Does changing position multiple times also incur performance issues? Bring the spawn rate back to 0.1, but change position twice.
Result: Yup. Check out move_bullets_3.
Insight: The more you move your collision boxes around, the worst it is. But really, the insight is to roll your own collision.

Architecture time: The question is, do we need to support weird bullets? Should a bullet track its own place in space? I think performance-wise it should be about the same
whether the bullet manager holds all the functions, or calls into the Sprites' scripts? If we do the latter, we can have the bullets track their own position and 
report on which screen quadrant they're in. Then you can override that logic for weird elongated or large bullets. But alternatively, maybe we don't need fancy bullets, and we should
treat every bullet as a point (or a ray).

Incidentally: After removing all the move functions but forgetting the last one at 1/4 speed: It looks like we can get to about 10k dumb bullets before we hit performance issues.
Which reminds me, I should make a framerate label.

Test 15: Dumb bullets and dumb collision. Loop through every bullet and check its distance to the player (but use DistanceSquaredTo). We're also bringing it back down to 2800 bullets.
Result: 10ms, and only 0.74ms is test_collision(). So doing spatial partitioning, quadtrees, etc. isn't strictly necessary.

Test 16: Out of curiosity, how much worse is distance_to than distance_squared_to?
Result: About the same. Perhaps not surprising. test_collision is a bit worse, 0.8-0.9ms instead of 0.7ms

We're now at the point where Process Time is the main slow thing, at 14ms at times. Visual Profiler is well under 16ms/frame, so I'm not sure what the bottleneck is.
Insight: The graph spikes when I move the game screen to my secondary monitor. I wonder how many tests this invalidates... If I don't move the screen, the game runs at a pretty constant
5ms, which seems pretty happy. I might not even need to do any fancy stuff with MultiMesh? We'll see how it looks once I try animated sprites and shaders.
Is this a refresh rate thing? See: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html

Test 17: Add a shader to the sprite. My Frame time is now 4-6... did I do something weird? Surely it's not faster than it was without a shader.

Test 18: Switched shader to screenspace shader. Still good.

Test 19: Switched the bullet to a QuadMesh MeshInstance2D. Performance is still good. TODO: Is a ColorRect a better choice here,
or is that weird because it's a Control node? What's the right object for this? I guess it's not common to have a
shader-only object.

I also took the time to make the bullet round and pretty, and to fix the bullet render order.

Test 20: Can we move the movement and cleanup logic back to the bullet? It would make it a lot easier
to build patterns. 
Result: Might be a bit slower. Might be worth it though? It says 2ms on Bullet._process, which isn't bad.

These fluctuating numbers on the Profiler makes it hard to really tell when the difference is 1ms. Is there
a better way?
I need to test this on a crappy computer as a confidence test.

I now moved back to dumb bullets. I want sprites, and I think the performance is better with dumb
bullets. Pushing forward for now, but it'll soon be time for a rewrite.

Just had a great idea for an experimental shmup: You drive a train, i.e. you're on rails, and you can only control your movement on
one axis. It'd take a lot of design to make that playable, but I've always found it interesting when you can dodge a pattern by moving
up/down instead of the obvious left/right. I think the rail orientation would change sometimes.

Test 21: Added hitboxes.gd to draw debug hitboxes. Unfortunately this tanks performance, from a stable 200fps down to 18fps.
Is there a way around this? Can I batch draw calls somehow? Can I draw to an image in memory, and then draw the image to the screen just once?

Test 22: Did some refactoring into the Bullet class, with the goal of making bullet management easier.
The performance tanks all at once at the 4000~ bullet mark. A bit of movement, a bit of test_collision.
Would C# be more performant?
